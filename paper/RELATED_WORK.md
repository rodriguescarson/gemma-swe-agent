# Related work: verified source list

**Verified on:** 2026-10-19.

**How it was checked:** each row was checked by opening its primary source: the arXiv abstract page (`arxiv.org/abs/...`), the official proceedings page, the official repository, or the author's own blog. Titles, authors, years and ids were copied from those pages, not filled in from memory. Two PDFs were read as text with `pdftotext`: arXiv 2607.08938, for its meta-agent passage, and the official ICLR 2025 slides for ADAS, to confirm the venue. Venue labels marked "per arXiv comments" come from the authors' own arXiv comments field; no proceedings page was opened for those.

**Caveat:** most pages were read through a summarising fetch tool. Spot-check any number before quoting it in the paper.

Relevance key: **A** = local screen vs hidden-set ordering, runtime, levers that do not merge. **B** = Harness² improver-model experiment, cost, transfer to unseen repos.

### Opened but left out of the table (cap of five extra works)

- **SWE-Effi: Re-Evaluating Software AI Agent System Effectiveness Under Resource Constraints.** Fan, Z. et al., 2025, arXiv:2509.09853, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09853. It scores SWE agents against token, cost, CPU-time and inference-time budgets and describes "expensive failures" on unsolvable tasks. Relevant to A's wall-clock argument.
- **"Why I built the Konwinski Prize".** Konwinski, A., blog post, 12 Dec 2024, https://andykonwinski.com/2024/12/12/konwinski-prize.html. A Kaggle-hosted, open-weights-only SWE-style contest whose test set is collected after the submission deadline. This is the closest Kaggle precedent for A. Its results (a winning score around 7.5%) and its compute limits appear only in news coverage. The Kaggle page (https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/konwinski-prize) did not render, so neither is verified from a primary source.

## Not found / renamed

All five unverified candidates exist. None is NOT FOUND, but three differ from how the review named them:

- **C1, Harness².** The guessed title is correct. arXiv renders it "Harness^2: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World", and the repo README writes "Harness²" and links arXiv 2610.14224. The README itself does not name the improver model. The paper's main editor is Claude Opus 4.8, and the models being improved are GLM-5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash.
- **C3, Self-Harness.** The full title is "Self-Harness: Harnesses That Improve Themselves". The review's description matches the abstract: failure analysis ("Weakness Mining"), minimal edits (bounded "Harness Proposal") and regression testing ("Proposal Validation").
- **C4, MESH-Harness.** The full title is "MESH-Harness: Self-Improving Agent Harnesses via Bandit-Guided Compositional Evolution". A standard web search for the bare name returned nothing; an extended search found arXiv 2610.05300. The "limited evaluation budget" description matches its abstract.
- **C2 and C5** match exactly. C5 is a blog post, not a paper.

Established works: none NOT FOUND. Some title and venue variants are recorded above so the citation uses the right version: DSPy, Agentless/FSE, ABC (arXiv "for Building" vs NeurIPS "in Building"), and SWE-bench-Live (the paper is titled "SWE-bench Goes Live!").

Not verified and not cited:

- **"Le et al. 2026".** A coding-agent study cited secondhand by arXiv 2609.24974 (Harness-Zero) on cross-harness transfer. Neither paper was opened.
- **K Prize results.** Seen only in news coverage.
- **SWE-Bench Pro pass rates.** Not on the abstract page.

The list above was compiled by a research subagent that read most pages through a summarising fetch tool. Two entries
that decide the paper's positioning were re-opened at arxiv.org/abs here:
- 2607.12227 "Rethinking the Evaluation of Harness Evolution for Agents" (Wang, Zhu, Hu, Yuan, Chen, Senthil, Hajishirzi,
  Tsvetkov, Smith, Dasigi, Xiao; v1 14 Jul 2026, v4 5 Oct 2026). CONFIRMED in the abstract: harness evolution "fails to
  outperform simple test-time scaling methods" under comparable budgets (Terminal-Bench 2.1), and evolving on verification

  figure in the subagent's summary; the abstract says only "limited generalization". Do not cite a number until the body
  is read here.
- 2607.08938 "Better Harnesses, Smaller Models: Building 90% Cheaper Agents via Automated Harness Adaptation" (Yang, Zhao,
  Wu, Kästner; 9 Jul 2026). CONFIRMED: small models + an adapted harness recover most frontier performance on business tasks
  (16 of 21 task-SLM pairs improved). NOT in the abstract: Gemma 4 as a target model, and the cheaper-improver-was-worse
  result; both are body claims from the subagent, unconfirmed here.
